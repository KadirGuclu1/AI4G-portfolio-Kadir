# Hackaton week 2
## SleepWindDown - Evening Routine with Screen Reduction

We built an automated n8n workflow that acts as a digital well-being coach for students and young adults who struggle to wind down at night because of heavy screen use. Every evening at 21:30, the workflow sends a short form asking about today's screen time, mood, and stress. An AI node analyzes these answers and generates a personalized wind-down routine: less screens before bed, light stretching, and a short reflection. Depending on the user's mood and stress level, they receive either a standard or an extra supportive tip by email. All responses and generated routines are logged in Google Sheets, so a user can look back on their own patterns over time.

The workflow is built around eight nodes. A Schedule Trigger fires daily at 21:30. A Form Trigger collects screen time, mood, and stress, plus an optional note. A Google Sheets node appends every answer to a log. An AI node analyzes the input and drafts a routine. An If/Condition node checks whether mood and stress fall in a healthy range, and depending on that outcome the flow branches into either a standard "good tip" or an extra "supportive tip," before a Gmail node sends the result to the user. Because the AI interprets the answers in context rather than judging screen time on its own, a student who reports high screen time from studying gets a different response than one who reports the same hours from late-night scrolling.

Our tool supports SDG 3: Good Health & Well-being, with a focus on mental health, stress awareness, and sleep-related well-being. It targets students and young adults (16-30 years) in the Netherlands and Europe, a group where 86.3% use digital devices within 30 minutes of going to bed and only 9.5% follow basic sleep hygiene advice, and where university students average around 5.4 hours of daily screen time with 61% reporting poor sleep quality. Rather than lecturing users about "bad" screen habits, SleepWindDown gives a small, private, daily nudge toward healthier evening routines. Because it runs automatically and logs data over time, the same tool keeps supporting a user's habits well beyond a single use.

The workflow does not diagnose or treat anything, and it says so explicitly. Every message reminds the user this is not a medical or diagnostic service, and anyone reporting persistently low mood or high stress is encouraged to contact a GP or other professional. It requires a working email address and a few seconds each evening to fill in the form, but no coding knowledge or account setup beyond the initial n8n and Google/Gmail credential configuration.
